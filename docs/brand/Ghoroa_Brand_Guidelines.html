01Brand Foundation

The word means home. The room proves it.

Ghoroa (ঘরোয়া) translates to "homely" — domestic, familiar, unguarded. The interior direction already earned that word: warm clay walls, cane and rattan seating, brass fixtures, arched thresholds, plants trailing off every shelf. The brand's only job is to not get in the way of that feeling — in print, on screen, in a bag riding on the back of a motorbike.

i.

Warm, not precious

Nothing about this brand should feel like it's trying to impress. It should feel like it's trying to make you comfortable. Texture and imperfection over polish and symmetry.

ii.

Handmade, not manufactured

Cane weaving, hand-thrown ceramics, brass that's been touched — the brand's textures and type choices should read as crafted, never templated or corporate.

iii.

Local, not generic-global

Bilingual by default, not as an afterthought. Bangla carries equal weight to English — this is a Dhaka home, not an international chain wearing a Bangla sticker.

03Typography

One serif with warmth, one sans with none.

Fraunces carries the personality — it has the same soft, slightly irregular character as the hand-thrown ceramics in the space. Work Sans stays out of the way for anything functional. Hind Siliguri is the mandatory Bangla pairing — set at equal visual weight, never smaller or secondary to the English.

04Signature Motif — The Arch

The one shape that's unmistakably this room.

Every rendering in the deck repeats the same architectural gesture: the rounded arch, in doorways, mirror frames, and niches. That's the brand's signature device — not a logo symbol bolted on top, but the actual shape of the space, reused as a container for photography, menu dividers, and the wordmark's negative space.

Use the arch as a photo crop, a menu section-divider, a QR-code frame, a stamp on the takeaway bag. Never as a decorative border on unrelated content — it should always be doing a job (framing, dividing, containing).

05Voice & Tone

Write like the host, not the menu printer.

Every line of copy — app notifications, menu descriptions, delivery texts — should sound like someone who's glad you came, not a system confirming a transaction.

Do

"Your rider's on the way — should be with you in about 20 minutes."

Don't

"Order #4471 status: OUT FOR DELIVERY."

Do

"Slow-braised overnight, finished with brown butter."

Don't

"Premium slow-cooked protein entrée."

Do

Bangla and English carry equal warmth — write the Bangla first when the dish is a home-style/traditional item, English first for the globally-styled ones.

Don't

Machine-translate English copy into Bangla as an afterthought.
